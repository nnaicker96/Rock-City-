# NEXT DOOR — WHO KNOCKED FOR YOU? | Design Brief
21 October 2026 • Source: explicit user instructions and five reference designs supplied in conversation.

## Status
User-directed concept established. Two generated trials were rejected. No approved series poster yet. The five user-uploaded Next Door reference designs are visual references, not evidence that every layout is approved. Original image binaries are conversation uploads; they are not yet stored in GitHub.

## Governing creative concept
Exact recurring headline: **WHO KNOCKED FOR YOU?**
The series shows everyday modern encounters: a friend, family member, colleague, customer or other person within reach. Each poster depicts a credible human interaction in a distinctive environment. **An actual, visible, physical door must be present in every composition and naturally belong to that environment.** It may be a canteen door, fitting-room door, shop door, or another real door. It does NOT require a person standing at the threshold or physically knocking. Do not substitute an abstract door metaphor for the physical door.
The image invites viewers to remember who approached, invited, prayed for or brought the gospel near to them. Do not explain the answer or add another slogan to the poster. No mandatory verse has yet been specified for the first modern-scene trial; do not invent one.

## Distinctive examples from user
- Two colleagues interacting in an office canteen, with the canteen door part of the room.
- People in a mall fitting-room setting, with a fitting-room door.
- People interacting in a shop, with the shop door.
- Friends and family in similarly believable everyday spaces.
These are options, not instructions to crowd all scenarios into one image.

## Visual reference observations (five uploaded posters)
Bright clear skies; vivid foliage; sunlit depth; rich reds, yellow/gold and black; large condensed editorial type; organic framing; cinematic richly detailed illustrated environments; recognisable Rock City Dome when relevant; red architectural door and multicolour Transformation butterfly appear in references. Keep coherent campaign identity but do not mechanically replicate the original red-door foreground layout or force the Dome/butterfly into every scene.

## Rejected trials — explicit anti-patterns
1. Café entrance: two men with mugs positioned around a gigantic red door, oversized angled text and decorative butterfly.
2. Office canteen: essentially the same giant diagonal headline/red door/coffee-chat composition moved indoors, with an added butterfly mural.
Both were rejected because the human scene lacked distinct storytelling, the door dominated rather than belonging naturally, the imagery leaned photorealistic instead of matching the richer illustrated references, and the second repeated the first despite feedback.
**Do not repeat this composition.** A different setting alone does not constitute a substantive revision.

## Theological alignment
The October–November Logos Core LAB's Movement 2, **SOMEBODY KNOCKED FOR ME**, asks: “Through whose ordinary obedience did the good news come within reach of me?” Primary anchors Romans 10:14–15 and John 1:40–46. The LAB explicitly honours ordinary invitations, prayers and conversations. NEXT DOOR remains October's evangelism theme, anchored in John 1:14, and the wider campaign question is “If Jesus moved into your neighbourhood, what would that look like?” The WHO KNOCKED FOR YOU? poster headline is a related series question, not a replacement for the original campaign question in other materials.

## Execution acceptance test
- Exact headline, correct spelling.
- Real, visibly present door that makes architectural sense for chosen setting.
- Believable interaction central to scene; not merely two people posed with coffee at a door.
- Strongly different staging and hierarchy from rejected trials.
- Faithful rich editorial Next Door design language and correct pillar palette.
- No unsolicited captions, verses, slogans, venue times or icons.
- Evaluate actual output before presenting; acknowledge any remaining deviations.
