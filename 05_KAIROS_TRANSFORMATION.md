# Kairos 2026–27 — Transformation

Source: Transformation Lab PDF(2).pdf, 50-page leaders’ pack, checked 3 October 2026. This page is a working summary, not a replacement for the full teaching.

## Meaning and direction

Transformation is God’s work within us, renewing our minds and forming the likeness of Christ. People intentionally participate through drawing near, discipleship and shared life. The theme is not a cosmetic change to programmes or an invitation to generic self-improvement.

**Revelation → Formation → Function** summarises the movement: what is received from God takes root in people and becomes visible in how they live and serve. God is constant; the change is in people.

## The whole movement

Evangelism draws people into an encounter with Christ and church life. Worship, discipleship and fellowship form one connected **Transformation Zone**. Ministry carries formed life into service and community change. Invitation and discipleship belong together from the beginning.

| Area | Established emphasis | What it means in practice |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Worship | Proximity to God | Prayer, Scripture, fasting and surrender; relationship with God |
| Discipleship | Intentional covenanting | Deliberate participation in formation and discipling relationships |
| Fellowship | Custodians of a Christlike culture | Shared life, care and responsibility for the culture we create |
| Ministry | Formation into function | Christlike life expressed in service, households and community |

## Worship: proximity

The emphasis is proximity rather than a relationship reduced to petitioning. Associated outcomes are alignment, clarity, power and unity. Intentional proximity addresses default drift through competing priorities, trials, temptations and individual pursuits.

The Prayer Wheel includes **Adoration, Confession, Thanksgiving, Intercession and Petition**. The Word is received as revelation that should become transformation. These practices nurture relationship; they are not mechanical formulas for obtaining results.

## Discipleship: covenanting

Covenanting is intentional commitment to the journey of formation. Passive attendance does not substitute for participation. Established vocabulary includes **Spiritual Vitality**, **Growth Mapping**, **Transformation Zone**, **Three Chairs** and the cascade **1 → 3 → 12 → 30 → 300**.

Keep those terms, but consult the complete source before explaining the precise chair roles or turning the cascade into a programme. The numbers are not an attendance forecast or a substitute for people being formed.

## Fellowship: culture

Fellowship makes Christlikeness visible in how people care, listen, respond and carry responsibility together. Leaders are custodians of the meaning behind the practice. Relational check-ins need genuine care and engagement; completing a round of answers is not the purpose.

## Ministry: formation into function

Service should express continuing formation. A capable volunteer still needs discipleship, fellowship and proximity to God. Shared ownership helps people understand purpose, carry responsibility and help others learn. Ministry reaches everyday relationships and communities as well as organised church activities.

## Transformation Engagement for leaders

The leaders’ pack develops these connected ideas:

- **Saturate ourselves in the Word:** receive and remain with what God is saying personally.
- **Be a reservoir, not a channel:** allow the Word to dwell within us so service grows from a life continually receiving from Christ.
- **Alignment before function:** understand the meaning before translating it into ministry activity.
- **Counsel that carries:** contribute insight while sharing responsibility for what grows.
- **Modelling, beyond mimicking:** embody the principle rather than only copying the outward practice.
- **People being formed, together:** structures support the people and their formation.

The pack describes personal receiving, shared understanding and cascade into the lives of those served. The recurring caterpillar, cocoon and butterfly imagery supports this formation journey.

## Logos Core

Logos Core is a distinct Rock City group that participates in the shared theological and leadership work of the Kairos year. Within Transformation, its working posture is **saturation before cascade**: leaders receive the Word personally, develop shared theological understanding, test formation implications, and only then translate the teaching into sermons, ministry environments, discipleship conversations and creative communication.

Logos Core LABs are therefore not merely sermon-planning meetings. They are formation-and-discernment spaces. A LAB should normally protect this movement:

1. **Revelation** — What is Scripture revealing about God, Christ, grace, holiness, Kingdom and the gospel?
2. **Formation** — What must be surrendered, renewed, confronted or cultivated in us?
3. **Function** — What does formed life now do in witness, worship, discipleship, fellowship, ministry and ordinary life?
4. **Cascade** — How can leaders carry the theological centre faithfully into their ministry context without reducing it to a slogan or activity?

## Scripture and translation

The leaders’ pack uses **KJV**. Key Transformation anchors in the wider work include **Romans 12:1–2** and **Romans 8:29**. Source passages used in the checked engagement section include Colossians 3:16; John 15:4–5; Galatians 5:22–25; Ezra 7:10; Philippians 2:1–5; 1 Timothy 4:12; and Ephesians 4:11–16.

Preserve the reference and translation specified for an individual task. Do not treat a broad list of passages as permission to substitute one for another.

## Translating the teaching

Keep Christ, Scripture, grace and formation central. Adapt the language and examples to the audience without replacing theology with motivation. Children need concrete stories and choices; youth need honest discussion; adult men can use practical parallels that illuminate the passage. A clever analogy is not itself the teaching.
