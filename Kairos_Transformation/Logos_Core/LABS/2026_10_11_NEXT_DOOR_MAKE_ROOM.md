# Logos Core LAB — October–November 2026
## NEXT DOOR → MAKE ROOM

**Kairos Year:** Transformation  
**October pillar:** Evangelism — NEXT DOOR  
**November pillar:** Worship — MAKE ROOM  
**Internal November formation metaphor:** The Rehearsal Room  
**Governing movement:** Revelation → Formation → Function

## Why these two months belong together

These are not two disconnected campaigns.

October asks: **Who needs Christ near them, and what kind of heart will move toward them?**

November asks: **How near is Christ being allowed to come to me, and what is His nearness forming in me?**

The two-month movement is:

**Christ comes near → Christ knocks → we open → we encounter Him → we are formed → we carry Christ → we open deeper rooms to Him.**

The source thesis is that Christ comes near enough to save us, remains near enough to form us, and sends us near enough to others that His life becomes visible through ours.

The public November theme is **MAKE ROOM**. “The Rehearsal Room” remains useful inside Logos Core as a theological metaphor for hidden formation: what is practised privately eventually becomes visible publicly.

# MONTH ONE — OCTOBER: NEXT DOOR

## Month thesis

Evangelism should not begin with pressure to perform evangelism. It begins with recovering the goodness of salvation, the God who comes near, and the value of the person who does not yet know Him.

**Formation aim:** move from evangelism as obligation to evangelism as overflow.

**Campaign anchor:** John 1:14.  
**Campaign question:** “If Jesus moved into your neighbourhood, what would that look like?”

“Next Door” is primarily a proximity idea, not merely a geography idea. Your next door is the person whose life God has placed within reach of yours.

## October theological movement

### Movement 1 — THE GOD WHO COMES NEAR

**Core question:** Before we talk about us going, what does Scripture reveal about the God who comes?

**Primary anchors:** John 1:14; Luke 19; Revelation 3:20.  
**OT conversation:** Exodus 25:8; Exodus 33; Genesis 3, handled carefully.

**Revelation**
The gospel begins in divine initiative. God is not the distant object of humanity's religious search. In Christ, the Word becomes flesh and dwells among us. Jesus enters homes, meals, streets and ordinary human life.

**Theological tension**
Evangelism can subtly become a story about our courage, technique or church programme. Scripture first makes it a story about God's movement toward people.

**Formation**
Recover wonder at grace. Before asking, “Who have I reached?”, ask, “How has God come near to me?”

**Function**
A people shaped by the nearness of Christ learn to move toward people rather than waiting for people to enter church space.

**LAB questions**
- What changes when evangelism begins with incarnation rather than instruction?
- Where have we reduced salvation to a decision without recovering the God who moved first?
- What does John 1:14 demand of a church that wants to embody good news locally?
- How does divine initiative protect evangelism from ego?

**Cascade guardrail**
Do not reduce “Jesus moved into the neighbourhood” to a campaign slogan. Incarnation is not merely friendliness; the eternal Word truly became flesh and dwelt among us.

---

### Movement 2 — SOMEBODY KNOCKED FOR ME

**Core question:** Through whose ordinary obedience did the good news come within reach of me?

**Primary anchors:** Romans 10:14–15; John 1:40–46.  
**OT conversation:** Isaiah 52:7; 2 Kings 7:9; 2 Kings 5.

**Revelation**
God dignifies ordinary messengers. Andrew brings Peter. Philip says, “Come and see.” The unnamed servant girl in Naaman's story speaks from an apparently insignificant position and becomes part of a chain of healing.

**Theological tension**
We celebrate dramatic conversions while overlooking the ordinary invitations, prayers, conversations and faithful people through whom God brought the gospel near.

**Formation**
Gratitude restores the felt value of salvation. Testimony is not self-display; it remembers grace and the human chain through which grace travelled.

**Function**
The believer becomes willing to be one faithful link in somebody else's story.

**LAB exercise**
Map your gospel chain: who prayed, invited, taught, corrected, modelled, stayed, spoke or brought you near enough to encounter Christ?

**LAB questions**
- What do our testimonies reveal about the ordinary means God uses?
- Why do we sometimes think our obedience is too small to matter?
- How can testimony cultivate gratitude without making the messenger the hero?
- Who may be waiting on the other side of my obedience?

---

### Movement 3 — THE HEART THAT KNOCKS

**Core question:** What makes witness more than compliance?

**Primary anchors:** 2 Corinthians 5:14–20; Luke 15.  
**OT conversation:** Jonah 4; Isaiah 6:1–8.

**Revelation**
Paul says the love of Christ compels. Isaiah's sending follows revelation and cleansing. Jonah exposes the possibility of carrying God's message while resisting God's heart.

**Theological tension**
A person can obey an evangelism programme and still remain unmoved by people. Command can produce participation; it cannot by itself produce compassion.

**Formation**
Ask God to form His heart in us: gratitude for grace, compassion for people, willingness to be interrupted, and freedom from superiority.

**Function**
Witness becomes prayer, listening, testimony, invitation, service and truthful speech flowing from love rather than quota.

**LAB questions**
- Can I carry God's message while resisting God's heart?
- What does Jonah reveal about correct theology with malformed compassion?
- What is the difference between conviction and guilt-driven mobilisation?
- What would it mean for Christ's love, not a campaign deadline, to compel us?

**Diagnostic**
If the crusade disappeared, would our people still move toward the people God has placed within reach?

---

### Movement 4 — NEXT DOOR: NEAR ENOUGH TO REACH

**Core question:** Who has God already placed within reach?

**Primary anchors:** Luke 10:25–37; Acts 16.  
**OT conversation:** Jeremiah 29:7; Ruth 2.

**Revelation**
Neighbour is not merely a category of similarity or convenience. Jesus destabilises the question “Who is my neighbour?” by showing neighbour-love as movement toward the person whose need has come within reach.

**Theological tension**
Churches can prefer distant mission because nearby mission is relationally costly. The “next door” person can see whether our gospel is embodied in patience, integrity, generosity and presence.

**Formation**
Allow God to confront avoidance, prejudice, busyness, indifference and the desire for evangelism without relationship.

**Function**
Identify actual people and actual spaces: family, workplace, school, street, WhatsApp contact, customer, colleague, friend.

**LAB questions**
- Who is close enough to experience my Christianity rather than merely hear it?
- Where does convenience determine whom I notice?
- What would good news look like before an invitation to an event?
- How do service and verbal witness belong together without one replacing the other?

## October landing

October should culminate not merely in increased invitations but in a changed evangelistic posture:

**I have received good news → I remember who carried it to me → Christ's heart is forming mine → I move toward somebody within reach.**

The crusade or outreach event is therefore an **overflow point**, not the theological endpoint.

# MONTH TWO — NOVEMBER: MAKE ROOM

## Month thesis

Worship is proximity to God that forms us into the likeness of Christ.

November turns inward after October's outward movement. If we are to carry Christ, we must ask what kind of life is being formed in His presence.

**Public theme:** MAKE ROOM.  
**Internal LAB metaphor:** THE REHEARSAL ROOM.

“Make Room” is not primarily about creating a better worship atmosphere. It is the invitation to open the spaces of attention, habit, desire, thought, identity and obedience to God.

## November theological movement

### Movement 1 — WHO ARE WE MAKING ROOM FOR?

**Core question:** How can I become like Someone I do not know?

**Primary anchors:** Philippians 2:5–11; John 1:29–34; John 3:30.  
**OT conversation:** Isaiah 6; Exodus 3; Job 42:5–6.

**Revelation**
Worship begins with God, not the worshipper. Perspective determines posture. Isaiah sees the Lord and is undone. Moses encounters holiness before assignment. John the Baptist's sense of self, influence and importance is reordered by seeing Christ.

**Theological tension**
We can worship a culturally comfortable projection of Jesus and then call becoming like that projection “Christlikeness.”

**Formation**
Biblical literacy belongs inside worship. The clearer the revelation of Christ, the more accurately desire, reverence, surrender and imitation can be formed.

**Function**
Scripture becomes more than sermon material. The church learns to behold Christ truthfully and order life around who He is.

**LAB questions**
- What image of Jesus is our congregation actually carrying?
- Which parts of the biblical Christ do we emphasise, and which do we domesticate?
- What happens to worship when God's holiness and grace are both taken seriously?
- How does “He must increase” confront spiritual ego?

---

### Movement 2 — OPEN THE REHEARSAL ROOM

**Core question:** What am I rehearsing in private that is forming who appears in public?

**Primary anchors:** Matthew 6:1–18; Romans 12:1–2; James 1:22–25.  
**OT conversation:** Psalm 51; 1 Samuel 16–17; Exodus 2–4.

**Revelation**
God is concerned with the hidden person, not only public religious expression. Jesus directly confronts prayer, fasting and giving performed to be seen. Romans 12 makes worship bodily and transformational.

**Theological tension**
We can rehearse Christian behaviour without becoming like Christ:
- learn the sound of prayer without communion;
- fast without reordered desire;
- sing surrender while protecting rooms from God;
- serve efficiently while remaining spiritually unformed;
- preach Scripture while drifting from the God of Scripture.

**Formation**
Make room for God in thought life, phone use, relationships, ambition, reactions, appetite, schedule, money and hidden habits.

**Function**
Public worship becomes the expression of a life being surrendered rather than a substitute for surrender.

**LAB questions**
- What can leaders become skilled at doing while avoiding formation?
- Which “rooms” are easiest to keep closed while remaining publicly functional?
- Where are we mimicking Christian culture instead of modelling Christ?
- What does Romans 12 do to a definition of worship limited to music?

---

### Movement 3 — PRACTISING PROXIMITY

**Core question:** How do practices serve relationship without becoming replacements for relationship?

**Primary anchors:** John 15; Luke 5:16; Romans 8:14.  
**OT conversation:** Exodus 33:7–11; 1 Samuel 3; Daniel 6:10.

**Revelation**
Abiding is relational dependence. Jesus withdraws to pray. Samuel learns to listen. Moses meets with God. Daniel's rhythm expresses allegiance rather than spiritual theatre.

**Theological tension**
Disciplines can be neglected because they feel legalistic, or practised mechanically as though repetition itself guarantees transformation.

**Formation**
Recover prayer, Scripture, fasting, gathering and fellowship as practices of nearness, listening, surrender and alignment.

**Prayer movement:** **Listen → Discern → Align.**

This sits alongside the established Prayer Wheel — Adoration, Confession, Thanksgiving, Intercession and Petition — rather than replacing it.

**Function**
Practices become trellises for relationship: structures that support life without being mistaken for the life itself.

**LAB questions**
- When does discipline become legalism?
- When does fear of legalism become an excuse for spiritual passivity?
- What does it mean to listen before petitioning?
- How do we test discernment biblically and communally?
- What in my life must change if I genuinely believe I have heard God?

**Guardrail**
Discipline serves relationship. Relationship does not exist to serve discipline.

---

### Movement 4 — HE MUST INCREASE

**Core question:** What does worship look like when Christ increasingly occupies the life we have made room in?

**Primary anchors:** John 3:30; Galatians 2:20; 2 Corinthians 3:18.  
**OT conversation:** Isaiah 6:8; 1 Samuel 3:10; Micah 6:8.

**Revelation**
Beholding and transformation belong together. Worship is not making God more present; it is the worshipper becoming increasingly attentive, surrendered, receptive and aligned to the God who is present.

**Theological tension**
We can mistake emotional intensity for spiritual formation. A powerful moment may be real and valuable, but the question remains: what is becoming different because we have drawn near?

**Formation**
Decrease is not self-hatred. It is the reordering of ego, desire, identity, ambition and control beneath the lordship of Christ.

**Function**
Worship becomes visible in obedience, holiness, service, Spirit-led decisions, neighbour-love and Christlike response.

**LAB questions**
- What evidence of proximity should appear on Monday?
- How is “He must increase” different from erasing personality or agency?
- What does a formed response look like under pressure?
- If our worship never alters our relationships, decisions or desires, what exactly are we rehearsing?

## November landing

November should culminate in a worship posture deeper than participation in a service:

**I see Christ more clearly → I open the hidden rooms → I practise proximity → my life is reordered → Christ becomes increasingly visible.**

# THE TWO-MONTH HANDOFF

## Door → Room

October ends at somebody else's door. November brings us back to our own.

**October:** Someone needs Christ near them.  
**November:** I must allow Christ near enough to form me.

This prevents two distortions:
1. **Evangelism without formation** — carrying a message our lives increasingly contradict.
2. **Formation without mission** — spiritual intimacy that never moves toward the people God loves.

The mature movement is circular rather than linear:

**Christ comes near → we are formed in proximity → we carry Him toward others → mission exposes where we still need formation → we make room again.**

# LOGOS CORE LAB RHYTHM

This material is two months of theological territory, not a mandatory eight-sermon schedule. For Logos Core, it can be worked through as **eight LAB movements** across October and November, combined or revisited as required.

For each LAB movement use:

1. **Saturate** — read the primary passage in context before discussing ministry application.
2. **Observe** — what does the text actually reveal?
3. **Name the tension** — where does this confront our assumptions, church habits or personal posture?
4. **Formation table** — what must be surrendered, renewed, cultivated or practised?
5. **Discussion** — use the LAB questions to expose real disagreement and reflection, not obtain the “church answer.”
6. **Function** — what does faithful embodiment look like?
7. **Cascade** — what must remain intact when this reaches the wider church?

## Cascade questions for every movement

- What is the one theological truth that cannot be lost?
- What false shortcut would flatten this into behaviour management?
- What should leaders embody before asking the church to embody it?
- What does this mean for adults, youth and children without changing the theology?
- What should be preached, practised, prayed, discussed or demonstrated?
- What would count as evidence of formation rather than mere participation?

# DOCTRINAL AND INTERPRETIVE GUARDRAILS

- John 1:14 is the central October anchor: keep incarnation central rather than using “moved into the neighbourhood” as a detached metaphor.
- Revelation 3:20 is addressed within a message to a church; do not build the whole doctrine of conversion on an isolated reading of the verse.
- Do not teach evangelism as earning favour with God or as proof of superior spirituality.
- Do not make testimony human-centred; Christ remains the saving actor.
- Do not equate worship with music.
- Do not imply spiritual disciplines manipulate God's presence.
- Do not equate emotional response with transformation.
- Do not frame “decrease” as worthlessness; Christian surrender is Christ-centred reordering, not destruction of personhood.
- Do not teach transformation as behaviour modification. The aim is formation into Christlikeness by God's work in us, intentionally participated in.
- Keep grace and intentionality together: God's initiative does not eliminate response; disciplined response does not manufacture grace.

# FORMATION OUTCOMES TO WATCH

By the end of October, leaders should be able to articulate:
- why the gospel is genuinely good news;
- how Christ's initiative shapes Christian mission;
- who helped bring Christ within reach of them;
- where guilt-driven evangelism differs from love-compelled witness;
- who their actual “next door” people are.

By the end of November, leaders should be able to articulate:
- who the biblical Christ is beyond cultural projection;
- what private patterns are forming them;
- why disciplines serve proximity rather than replace it;
- what surrender and renewed thinking mean in ordinary life;
- how worship should become visible as Christlike function.

The goal is not that Logos Core possesses eight polished talks. The goal is that the theology has become alive enough in the leaders that what is eventually preached, taught and cascaded comes from the reservoir.
